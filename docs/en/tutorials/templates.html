Templates: when to use each

Guide to the kit's eight templates: what each one answers, who writes it, when to open and close it, and how they relate.

01Positioning

What this is

The templates live in templates/pt-br/ and templates/en/ in the kit, in mirrored versions; setup writes language to .harness/project.yaml and selects the folder. In the consumer project, each one is copied to .harness/ and filled in. Five describe work (PRD, Story, Task/Bug, ADR, RFC); three preserve state (MEMORY, EPOCHAL, RISKS). This guide explains which one to use, who writes it, and when to open and close it.

Facts, hypotheses, and decisions are marked as such. Paths are relative to the project. No secrets. Every acceptance criterion is “When X, then Y” and points to the scenario or test that proves it. Evidence exists only with an explicit state: passed, failed, or not-run. Typechecking does not prove behavior.
The three memory records are written only by the Coordinator. All other roles report facts, evidence, and incidents; they do not edit MEMORY.md, EPOCHAL.md, or RISKS.md.
HTML comments inside templates are filling guidance. They are removed from the final file.

02PRD

Product · why and what

It answers why to do something and what to deliver, without saying how. A good PRD can be satisfied by three different technical designs. The product owner writes it, with the product-discovery role through /dev-harness:discover. It closes when the requirements and acceptance criteria are approved and the stories have been derived. Template: templates/<lang>/PRD.md.

In scope

  • Pain or opportunity without clear scope and acceptance criteria.
  • Problem with evidence, users, and scenarios; an outcome with a measurable signal.
  • Numbered requirements (RF), acceptance criteria for each requirement, and constraints that limit the solution.
  • Discarded alternatives, risks, and pending decisions with an owner.

Out of scope

  • Architecture, a library, or an internal flow. That belongs in an ADR or a plan.
  • Scope that is already clear and fits in one story: skip the PRD and open the story with its origin linked.
  • Estimation and priority. Keep them in the profile or project kanban.

03Story

Product · what behavior

A deliverable, end-to-end testable behavior. It is written by product-discovery or implementation-planner. The Given / When / Then scenarios become tests; the technical impact table supports decomposition into tasks and role selection. It closes when every acceptance criterion has evidence that it passed. Template: templates/<lang>/STORY.md.

In scope

  • A PRD requirement that needs to become something verifiable.
  • Happy path, relevant error, and empty or boundary case.
  • Impact on data, contracts, interfaces, integrations, dependencies, and security.
  • Verification strategy beyond the task tests.

Out of scope

  • Behavior that does not fit in a few tasks. Split it before planning.
  • Implementation steps and files. That belongs in the task.
  • More than one story in the same file.

04Task and Bug

Execution · how to execute a slice

The dispatch an agent receives. Everything it needs to start without asking is in the file or linked. Tasks and bugs use the same template: the task fills in the scope section; the bug fills in the defect section. It is written by implementation-planner (task) or debugger (bug). It lives in .harness/tasks/<id>/TASK.md. Template: templates/<lang>/TASK.md.

In scope

  • One-sentence objective, with the out-of-scope boundary stated explicitly.
  • Technical context: candidate files, local pattern to follow, contracts, decisions, and incidents.
  • Environment changes: library to install, update, or remove; environment variable; reversible migration; configuration.
  • Step-by-step plan with checks, a test table covering every acceptance criterion, and a result of passed / failed / not-run.
  • Bug: literal symptom, reproduction, expected versus observed behavior, cause marked as hypothesized or confirmed, and severity.

Out of scope

  • Invented behavior when a decision is open. Stop and report it.
  • More than two unsuccessful correction rounds. Return it to the Coordinator with evidence.
  • Closing a bug without reproduction. Leave it inconclusive and state the gap.
  • Commit, push, or deploy without explicit authorization.

05ADR

Decision · what was decided and why

A durable decision that is expensive to reverse, with context, options, choice, consequences, and a review condition. It is written by solution-architect; the owner approves it. It has the same form produced by the architecture-decisions skill. It closes with accepted status and is reviewed when the review condition occurs. Template: templates/<lang>/ADR.md.

In scope

  • Choice of database, framework, public contract, authentication model, or boundary between components.
  • Always include the keep as is option.
  • A preference stated as a preference, not as a requirement.
  • A check that proves it works and a signal that it is failing.

Out of scope

  • Cheap, local choice. Keep it in the task plan.
  • Complete solution design. That belongs in an RFC or a plan.
  • ADR without a review condition. It becomes permanent by accident.

06RFC

Decision · proposal before building · optional

It proposes a broad technical change and collects objections before building. Once approved, it generates an ADR for the durable decision and stories for the work. In solo development with agents, the function of collecting objections barely exists; an ADR with proposed status is usually enough. Template: templates/<lang>/RFC.md.

In scope

  • Technical change without a PRD: broad refactor, infrastructure migration, or replacement of a central library.
  • Impact beyond the author and expensive to reverse.
  • Adoption plan in slices, each with proof.
  • Dated discussion; a resolved objection remains marked, not deleted.

Out of scope

  • User pain. That belongs in a PRD.
  • Small decision. Go directly to an ADR.
  • An RFC as a prerequisite for every change. It is optional.

07Memory: MEMORY, EPOCHAL, and RISKS

State · Coordinator only

Three files in the consumer project, initialized by /dev-harness:setup from templates/. None of them closes. MEMORY.md stores the current execution state and is trimmed by /dev-harness:consolidate-memory. EPOCHAL.md receives the complete copy of each previous memory. RISKS.md stores serious incidents and prevention; resolved incidents remain.

In scope

  • MEMORY: objective, authorized scope, current decisions, blockers, evidence, next step, and brief history.
  • EPOCHAL: batches with ID, date, and time zone; comments outside the original block.
  • RISKS: incident with date, severity, files, critical rule, cause, state, and prevention.

Out of scope

  • Writing by any role other than the Coordinator.
  • Loading EPOCHAL without a concrete need or explicit request.
  • Hypothetical risk in RISKS. Only a known incident belongs there.
  • Consolidation that trims the record before checking the archived batch.

08Journey

Engineering flow using the kit commands. Not every task goes through every stage: a bug goes directly to fix; a small edit goes directly to build with a task.

  1. Discover a request or pain point becomes a PRD with requirements and acceptance criteria.
  2. Stories one per requirement, with scenarios and technical impact.
  3. Understand the scout maps the affected area without requiring an index.
  4. Plan the planner splits the work; the architect joins if there is a decision that is expensive to reverse and writes the ADR. The Coordinator consults MEMORY and, in a risk area, RISKS.
  5. Tasks one per slice, with environment, tests, and acceptance criteria.
  6. Build the implementer delivers the slice with checks run.
  7. Verify and review QA and the reviewer assess the stabilized state. Up to two correction rounds; then replan.
  8. Release and handoff readiness without publishing; state that can be carried into the next session.

10Composed packages: how the files connect

A PRD generates several stories, one per requirement. A story generates several tasks covering its acceptance criteria. An ADR constrains tasks and originates from a PRD, story, RFC, or incident. A task delivers its result and evidence to the Coordinator, who writes it to MEMORY; a serious incident goes to RISKS, and prevention from RISKS flows back to stories and tasks. A solid line means derivation or writing; a dotted line means reference or consultation.

11Mapped vs. open

Mapped

  • Eight templates per language in templates/pt-br/ and templates/en/, mirrored versions.
  • .harness/tasks/<id>/ and the three memory records defined by the product plan.
  • Task result format matches the response format for skills.

Open

  • Directories prd/, stories/, adr/, and rfc/ are a suggested convention, not a decision in the plan.
  • Whether to keep or discard the RFC as a first-line template.
  • Separate templates for plan, evidence, review, and handoff: a slice uses TASK.md; evidence goes in .harness/tasks/<id>/evidence/; handoff is the /dev-harness:handoff command. Stack profiles exist in profiles/ (English).
